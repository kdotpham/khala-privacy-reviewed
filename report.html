3. Buterin and Durov both place privacy in core infrastructure

The founders of Ethereum and Telegram, a messaging app with over a billion monthly users, reach the same conclusion from different starting points.

Vitalik Buterin

Co-founder

Ethereum

“Privacy is not about standing apart. It’s about standing together.” Why I support privacy, 14 April 2025

Why it matters

Control over information is power, so privacy protects decentralisation, personal freedom and institutions such as the secret ballot.

Where it belongs

Privacy belongs in the protocol, covering payments, application activity, chain reads and network-level anonymity.

Current gap

Much of the proposal remains a roadmap that wallets and applications would need to implement.

Pavel Durov

Founder and CEO

Telegram, over 1B monthly users

“Telegram would rather exit a market than undermine encryption with backdoors and violate basic human rights.” Telegram channel, 21 April 2025

Why it matters

Encryption protects everyday communication and personal safety, and any access built for authorities is also available to attackers.

Where it belongs

The product should exclude backdoors, even if that means withdrawing from a market.

Current gap

Telegram’s default Cloud Chats are not end-to-end encrypted; only Secret Chats are.

Implication Both argue for privacy in systems people already use, making wallet and application adoption central to how widely it reaches users.

7. Privacy demand alone does not set token value

Investors value privacy most when it is held as money: Zcash and Monero are worth about $31B together, and Venice reaches $1.1B because customer revenue buys back its token.

Grass and Helium, at $486M and $85M, show what networks that sell bandwidth and coverage can be worth once customers pay, which makes them reference points for UR (SN25) without setting its fair value.

12. Aztec’s private rollup is live, but $12M of stake secures only $58K of user funds

Each transaction is proved on the user’s device and settled on Ethereum by staked sequencers and provers, so AZTEC is needed both to operate the network and to pay its fees, yet only $58K of user funds have arrived and v5 carries a critical proving bug disclosed on 7 August.

What it sells Private smart contracts on Ethereum. Apps written in Noir can keep balances, logic and state hidden while still calling public contracts, so one app can mix private and public steps.

How value reaches the token Users must convert AZTEC into Fee Juice to transact, and the congestion share of each fee is burned. Each sequencer seat needs 200,000 AZTEC, so more usage and more operators both pull on the token.

What has to be true v6 must fix the critical proving bug disclosed on 7 August, which L2BEAT says puts funds and contract state at risk, and apps must bring user funds far above $58K for fees to matter next to rewards.

Supply risk Only 29% of the 10.35B supply circulates, the Foundation unlocks on 13 November, governance can mint up to 20% of supply a year, and rewards of 400 AZTEC per slot add to circulating supply.

ProversProving is permissionless. Provers produce one validity proof for an epoch of blocks and post it to Ethereum, earning 30% of block rewards and of unburned fees.Aztec documentation, economics and network
Ethereum settlementA verifier contract on Ethereum checks each epoch proof. Once it verifies, the state change is final and inherits Ethereum’s security.Aztec documentation; L2BEAT, 16 October 2026
